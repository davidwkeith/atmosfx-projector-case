# Design log

Chronological record of decisions, including dead ends, so the reasoning survives a change of tools.

## Requirements (from the owner)

- Waterproof case for a small projector and a Raspberry Pi 3 that controls an AtmosFX pumpkin effect
- Sits on the ground; projector on an internal ball joint for easy aiming
- Aiming should be as quick as possible (it is the primary setup task)
- A single power cord for the box; the projector uses a DC brick that can be modified to feed both projector and Pi; the brick goes inside the case

## Decisions and dead ends

- **Sealed vs rain-proof.** Sealing overheats projectors. AtmosFX says any enclosure must allow air circulation; a forum builder who put a projector in a louvered plastic tub felt it would just melt it. So: rain-shedding louvers, floor intake, exhaust fan.
- **Mini ball head, not a printed ball joint.** Commercial heads are cheap and lock reliably; printed friction joints creep. Rating matters: the Tilta mini head is rated 1.5 kg, the projector is about 0.72 kg. A heavy-duty head (Kessler low-profile, about 3.5 in tall) would push the base past the 210 mm Z limit and force a horizontal split.
- **Tripod insert removed** in v0.2 when the case became ground-standing.
- **Quarters to halves.** With a 260 mm projector the box needed four tiles. With the real projector, front/rear halves are enough. The divider sits at the seam.
- **Aim hatch.** Options weighed: tool-free lid latches (still lift the whole lid), a side hatch with screws, a keyhole cover. Keyholes won: nothing to unscrew, cover hangs on four M4 bolts, lift up and off. Hood is a 45 deg wedge (no supports). It first collided with the cover top and then with the lid skirt; shrunk to 10 mm and lifted; verified clear.
- **Floor intake.** Wall louvers moved out of the way of the hatch; intake now comes through slots in the raised floor. Risk: ground splash. Keep off wet ground.
- **Power.** Mains inside is the least safe option but the owner asked for it. Mitigations: barrier wall, GFCI, fuse, cord grip, PETG/ASA. If the owner changes their mind, v0.6 (brick outside) is the safer variant: revert `power_shelf` and `gland_d`.
- **Review fixes (v0.8).** The keyholes had the big end above the bolt, so gravity would have released the cover; now the narrow end is up and the hood sits `kh_drop` higher so the cover can lift. The AC cord entered below the shelf and ran past the Pi; the barrier only existed above the shelf, so the gland moved up to the AC side. The barrier (25 mm) was shorter than the brick (32 mm). The lid seam lands over the projector and cannot move behind the divider (the front lid tile would be about 258 mm), so it became a 45 deg scarf with a rib under the roof; a horizontal shiplap would have needed supports on the rear tile. The sloped roof only met the gasket at the rear edge, so the lid gained a flat land. Owner chose fuses on both AC and DC.
- **Aim range.** The new aim sweep in `check_clash.sh` showed the claimed +/-15 deg never fit: the ball pivot is about 90 mm below the top corners, so tilt swings them about 20 mm into the window frame or the divider. Gaps grew to 30/26 mm (base_front 241 mm). 15 deg pan and tilt together would need about 254 mm, so combined aim is limited to 10 deg. Setting the case roughly on target covers the rest.
- **Stock pane.** The pane was 96 x 76 mm, not a stock size. It is now a parameter set to 3 x 5 in (127 x 76.2 mm, 1/8 in thick) and the window is derived from it, 111 x 60 mm, which also gives the light cone more room when panned. 4 x 4 in would push the frame above the base rim; 4 x 6 in is wider than the space between the front lid posts.
- **Audio.** Owner wants an amp inside and speakers behind the projection on standard speaker wire. HiFiBerry Amp4 HAT: I2S DAC plus stereo class D amp on one board, runs from the 12-24 V splice and powers the Pi, so the buck converter goes. The Pi 3's analog jack is too noisy to feed an amp. Speaker zip cord is flat and won't seal in a PG gland, so it goes down a 15 mm floor chimney and out under the case. The shelf rose 5 mm to clear the HAT; the louvers and AC gland above it moved with it.
- **Pi sleds.** Owner wants multiple Pi generations. The Pi 3B/3B+, 4B and 5 share the 85 x 56 board and 58 x 49 hole pattern, so the sled is mainly a fixed interface plus easy removal. The Zero 2 W (58 x 23) is the real outlier. Lift-out from the top was chosen over a rear slide-out door: no new wall opening to keep dry. Sleds carry only the Pi and HAT; power and speaker wires unplug. Aligning every board's GPIO edge keeps the Amp4 in one place; the Zero sled supports the overhanging half of the HAT. The Pi 5 with Active Cooler needs a taller stack, so the shelf rose to 60 mm and the AC gland and pass-through now follow the shelf.
- **Startle scares.** Customer research: AtmosFX sells a motion-sensor player for buffer-plus-scare clips, and people build the same on Pis. The PIR goes in the rear wall because the case faces the projection surface, so visitors approach from behind it. A PIR can't see through the acrylic window (it blocks IR), so the dome sits in its own hole under a hood. Home automations can trigger scares too, so a remote or existing sensor works without the built-in one. Multi-projector sync was left for later.
- **Printability pass.** The base prints upright, and the floor was floating 6 mm up on two ribs and corner pads, a 148 mm bridge across the whole floor; it now stands on eight front-to-back ribs. The hatch became a 45 deg diamond (still 120 mm wide at the ball head). The window can't take a pointed top because the pane has to cover it, so two 0.8 mm snap-out ribs split its 111 mm bridge into thirds. The divider pass-through got a gabled top.
- **Projector power.** Owner chose HDMI-CEC, plus a fallback for projectors without it: a relay on the DC + line and an IR LED sending the remote's power code after the relay closes.
- **Heat.** The Pi zone held the brick, the amp and possibly a Pi 5 with only passive vents, and the original fan's power and control were never specified. Now both zones have 12 V PWM fans driven by the Pi from temperature sensors, the shelf has vents on its low-voltage side so heat from below reaches the Pi-zone fan, and the upper right-wall louvers gave way to that fan. The fan bodies are now in the aim sweep. A top-level variable used before its definition (`pi_fan_z` before `shelf_zz`) came back as undef; the new clash check caught it.
- **Insect screen.** A warm vented box outdoors invites wasps and spiders. Printed mesh is too coarse, so real screen is glued into printed caps over each louver bank and the floor intake, and clamped behind the fans. The first cap layout put a screw tab behind the rear lid post where no driver could reach; the tabs moved to top and bottom.
- **Tripod.** Owner wants the whole case tripod-mountable. 3/8-16 is the heavy-head standard and 1/4-20 fits quick-release plates, so both are fitted, in a solid pad flush with the rib bottoms so a plate seats flat and the ribs carry the load. The 3/8 insert is longer than the floor is thick, so it gets a small boss inside. Its position is an estimate of the centre of mass.
- **Dew.** Considered a pane heater switched on dew point; owner decided against it. The visor and fan airflow are the only defence against condensation on the window.
- **Keyhole leak, resolved by the diamond hatch.** With the square opening, the wide ends of the upper keyholes sat over the opening. The studs are at the square's corners (+/-56, +/-56), so the nearest wide end is about 98 mm (|y|+|z|) from the centre, far outside the diamond's 60 mm edge: every keyhole now sits over solid wall.
- **Base seam.** The lid's scarf doesn't transfer: the base prints upright, so a 45 deg scarf through 183 mm of height would shift 183 mm in Y, and any lap crossing the seam runs into the divider, which sits right at it. Instead the rear tile has 6 mm collars inside its side walls, and four M3 screws from the projector side pull it against the divider, which also aligns the halves. The collars stop below the shelf ledges and stay clear of the sled.
- **Lid screws.** The roof counterbores collected rain. A raised cone around each screw can't be printed, because the lid prints roof-down, so the screws moved to the skirt sides: horizontal M3s into blocks in the base wall tops, replacing the four full-height posts (which also frees the interior and the shelf corners). Slotted holes allow pressing onto the gasket; spacer rings keep the skirt from flexing across the lid_clr gap.
- **Anchoring.** Stake and lock tabs outside the walls would push the front tile past the bed's 210 mm axis (it's already 201 mm with the hatch hood). Instead, four sealed stake tubes stand inside the corners, like the speaker chimney. Stakes go in from inside, and with the lid on security screws they can't be pulled. That deters theft and resists wind without any change to the footprint.
- **Licensing.** Design and docs stay CC BY-SA 4.0; the Pi software is MIT, which suits code better and is compatible with matter.js (Apache-2.0).
- **CI.** The workflow now also runs check_clash.sh and the Pi tests. It uses Ubuntu's apt OpenSCAD (older CGAL build); the volume-based check doesn't depend on the backend's output format.
- **Light cone.** The body sweep never checked the light itself. Modelling the image pyramid for a 1.4:1 16:9 projector showed the 60 mm-tall window clipped past +8 deg of tilt, and only +4 deg with an upward-offset lens: bad, since tilting up is the main use. Fixes, each measured: a 4 x 5 in pane placed as high as the base rim allows (frame screws moved to its sides so the frame hugs the pane), the lid's front skirt notched over the window (its bottom edge sat 2 mm into the window), and a 30 mm visor with a 4 mm drip lip (the 12 mm lip was the next obstruction). Result: +/-12 deg tilt with a centred lens (check: `light-cone`), +10/-18 deg with a fully offset one.
- **Plugs behind the projector.** The 26 mm rear gap had no room for plugs. A keep-out for right-angle plugs (15 mm) in the aim sweep needed a 32 mm rear gap; the front gap came down from 30 to 26 mm without clipping anything (the lens now sits nearer the window), so `base_front` is 243 mm, 7 mm under the bed.
- **Slicing.** PrusaSlicer's CLI with the bundled MK3S profiles gives about 82 h and 1.25 kg in total, 37 h of it `base_front` (27 h at 0.30 DRAFT). A fit coupon (1.5 h) comes first. STLs moved from git to Release assets because every rebuild added megabytes of history.
- **Third review.** One version for the repo (the Pi package said 0.1.0 under tag v0.8.0, which would confuse `videofx-update`). The tripod insert moved from y 12 to 5 after computing the centre of mass from part centroids and slicer weights (about 2.6 kg in all). Added an optional hatch lock screw into the solid wall/floor corner, V-0 filament for the power shelf, cord routing for trick-or-treaters, an ordering plan with rough costs, and a bring-up checklist. Colima (open-source Docker) runs the pi-gen image build on the Mac.
- **Clash script.** The OpenSCAD snapshot uses the manifold backend, which never prints "Volumes: 1", so zero-volume contact (the shelf on its ledges) read as a clash. The script now measures intersection volume. It also used to report CLASH when OpenSCAD failed to run.
- **Fourth review: things the clash script couldn't see.** It only checks what is modelled, and no cable was. (1) The Pi's HDMI edge sits 9.5 mm behind the divider and the pass-through started 22 mm above the board, so no plug could go in; the pass-through now opens down to the sled and spans every board's port (`pass_x`), a straight plug goes through it and the cable loops under the projector, and `pi_hdmi_plug` is a keep-out in `check_clash.sh` (`hdmi-*`). The Zero 2 W is not in that check: its HAT support post stands 9 mm from the port centre, so it needs a slim plug. (2) The outer foot ribs stopped 5.5 mm short of the side walls, leaving a strip of floor the full length of the case printing in mid-air; the outer ribs now sit under the walls whatever the width (`ribs_x`), with the longest bridge asserted. (3) The pane rebate's flat ceiling was an unsupported edge 128 mm wide that could sag into the pane clearance; it is now 45 deg (the frame covers it, silicone fills it). (4) The gland entered 15 mm above the shelf, pointing at the side of the brick 16 mm away; it is now above the brick (`cord_dz` 46, asserted against `brick_h` and the lid skirt). (5) Nothing checked bed fit: `base_front` is 243 of 250 mm, so a projector 7 mm deeper than the guess would have broken the split silently. Each tile is now asserted against `bed`. Still open, waiting on the brick's measurements: the shelf's AC side has no room for the IEC plug (the brick's ends are 5 and 3 mm from the wall and barrier) or the inline fuse holder. Left alone: the tripod inserts go in from below, so the screw pulls them out of the plastic rather than into it; turning them round gains little without a flanged insert or T-nut, which is a hardware choice.
- **Fourth review: the Pi software.** Read against upstream sources, with nothing run on a Pi. First boot would have stopped at the partition step (`parted -s resizepart` on the mounted root asks a question and script mode takes that as failure; confirmed in a trixie container, replaced with `sfdisk`). The over-temperature path had several ways not to fire: no trip without a projector-zone reading (and the sensor ID defaults to empty), a failed state save aborting power-off before the projector was touched, the relay left closed when the service stopped or crashed, and fans not driven at all with Cooling off. All four are fixed, and a lost sensor now trips after 60 s. Still true: only the relay modes cut power; CEC can only ask. Also fixed: the IR power toggle sent again on a second "on" (which would switch a `relay-ir` projector off at sunset if it was already on), no picture when the projector connects after mpv starts (playback now waits for the HDMI sink and restarts when it appears), the web page refusing on-link IPv6 clients, an mpv option deny-list that let the page write any file (now an allow-list, plus `ProtectHome`), unlimited password guesses, the schedule not catching up after a power cut, mpv never restarted after giving up, and `READY=1` tied to Matter being online. Left for hardware: fan PWM on a Pi 5, whether the fbdev blank works once mpv is idle, CEC details.
- **Projector EDID (October 2026).** First hardware fact. The projector plugged into a MacBook reports a generic STK "S2-TEK TV" EDID: 1080p60 native, 8-bit, 150 MHz TMDS ceiling, 2-channel LPCM, DPMS standby flags, and an HDMI vendor block with CEC physical address 2.0.0.0. So the input is real HDMI and the `hdmi-off` fallback has something to work with, but CEC itself is still unproven (the address is mandatory for any HDMI sink; a Mac cannot speak CEC). The Pi should output 1080p60. Nothing about the case geometry changed: the EDID says nothing about size, lens or port positions.
- **Bugs hit:** shell `brace expansion` failing under sh (use bash), top-level variable used before definition (`lid_seam`/`yfl`), phony `make stl` clashing with the `stl/` directory (target is `parts`), tiny preview renders from `--viewall`.

- **Tkisko TO2 fit (v0.9).** The owner's projector is 165 wide with the lens on the long face, not 135 wide on the short face. With the 25 mm side gaps the base is 221 wide (231 with the hatch hood), past the 210 mm bed axis the old way round. Shrinking the side gap failed: the projector-zone fan body stands 14 mm proud of the right wall inside that gap, so even zero pan needs 15 mm, and the hatch hood takes another 10. The body is also 42 mm shorter, which brings the front base tile under 210 mm, so the front tiles now print end-on (X along the 250 mm axis) and the lid seam moved from 230 to 200 mm from the visor tip so `lid_front` fits the same way. The short throw (0.95 from the manual's image sizes) widens the light cone: it clips the window frame past 10 deg pan, so pan got its own limit (`pan_max`), with tilt still 15 and the combined corner 8 (the rear plugs reach the divider past that now the pivot sits 19 mm right of centre). Losing 10 mm of body height dropped the lid skirt onto the hatch hood; the diamond cannot go lower (its tip is 2 mm above the floor), so it shrank from 120 to 108 mm. The off-centre window frame overlapped the front lid-screw block by 0.35 mm, so that station moved 2 mm back. The label reads DC 21 V 3 A: the Amp4 is fine on 21 V, the fans must be 24 V parts, and the stock brick has no headroom for the Pi and amp.
